# Memo skeleton v3 — "Should harm affect the AI dividend's allocation rule?"

*Working title; final title names the decision, not the method.*

**Target length: 4 pages + references. Audience: a senior, time-constrained, non-technical reviewer at the Windfall Trust. Style: plain, descriptive sentences. Every section opens with its conclusion. No formal notation anywhere in the main text.**

**The spine (say it in four sentences):** Population share is the baseline allocation because AI rents are treated as a commons — an equal claim per person; this is assumed, not proven, and the memo says so. Weighting allocation by harm instead is the deviation, and the deviation must justify itself. It fails twice: current indices cannot measure who is actually hurt — and what they can measure shows harm is largest where cushions are deepest — and delivery is weakest exactly where a harm rule would send the most. Recommendation: allocate by population, escrow the shares of low-reachability countries, fund delivery rails from the escrowed flow, release on measured reachability.

**Scenario vocabulary (used everywhere, defined once):** all sensitivity in the memo runs on exactly three named scenarios — *cautious*, *central*, *fast* — defined in the implementation plan. No other ranges appear anywhere.

## Page 0 — Summary (half page, self-contained)

Leads with the sequencing finding, not the allocation verdict. A reader who stops here can state the question, the two failures, and the mechanism.

1. **The finding up front.** The binding constraint on an AI dividend is not the allocation formula but delivery: run $100 of Indonesia's population-share allocation through its delivery system and a material fraction does not arrive; the median low-income country loses far more. *(The "one of the better systems at its income level" clause appears here ONLY if the floor gate passes — the summary uses the same locked wording variant as §3.)* The design answer is sequencing — escrow and rails — not a different formula.
2. **The allocation question, honestly framed.** The reader may already favor per-capita allocation; this memo's product is the reason and the sequence. Population share is the commons baseline (assumed, with a source); harm-weighting is the deviation examined here, and it fails its own feasibility test.
3. **Failure one (measurement).** Existing exposure indices rank rich countries first — the tempting rule would allocate toward Boston before Jakarta — and after passing exposure through country-specific filters, remaining harm concentrates where cushions (formal contracts, unemployment insurance) are deepest. A harm rule, honestly measured, compensates the well-cushioned.
4. **Failure two (delivery).** Whatever the formula, arrival is capped by identification and payment rails, weakest where a harm rule would send the most — and material even in Indonesia.
5. **The mechanism.** Population allocation; shares of countries below a reachability threshold held in escrow; rails funded from the escrowed flow through existing multilaterals; release on measured reachability. One sentence of numbers honesty: magnitudes are transparent second-best judgments with named sources, pending formal estimates (§4).

## §1 — The decision and the baseline (½ page)

**Opening sentence:** "The most consequential open choice in an AI dividend is the allocation rule, and this memo tests the tempting deviation from the obvious baseline — give more where AI hurts more — and finds it fails on feasibility."

- **Baseline in two sentences (compressed principles menu):** population share treats AI rents as a commons: an equal claim per person. That justification is assumed here, not defended — [one Windfall-adjacent source] — so the question the memo answers is narrower and empirical: does harm-weighting clear its feasibility bar?
- **The shrinkage pre-empt, one sentence:** discounting a harm key toward the population baseline does not rescue it — a confidence-scaled version of an indefensible key is still indefensible, with false precision added; the honest commitment is to revisit when a defensible key exists (§4 names what that would take).
- **Rent incidence, one paragraph (framing only):** rents accrue overwhelmingly to a small set of firms in a few jurisdictions; collection happens at source. Consequence: the whole recipient-side question is allocation and delivery. (Anchor per implementation plan; concentration of development/deployment, labeled as framing, not a rent estimate.)
- **The method in one worked sentence, in words, no notation:** "Of the workers an index flags as exposed, only some are in jobs where AI can do most of the tasks, only some of those work in firms that will adopt this decade, and how the remainder splits between formal and informal work decides who bears it with what cushion — realized harm is the flagged share passed through those filters, each a transparent scale factor with its own source." *(Numeral "three filters" is NOT hard-coded; the implementation plan's embed check decides whether the task filter exists, and the drafting agent uses the matching wording variant.)*
- One-line method credential + link: "I have applied the same discipline — scale a measured headline to what it plausibly touches, let the decision follow — to a different question here: [DIV memo URL]."

## §2 — Failure one: harm cannot be measured for allocation, and measured honestly it inverts (1 page)

**Opening sentence:** "The first problem with allocating by harm is the ranking the data actually produce: every existing exposure index scores rich countries highest, and correcting for what indices miss makes the inversion worse, not better."

- **The gradient sentence:** advanced economies ~60% exposed, emerging markets ~40%, low-income countries lower still [source per plan] — so the compensation rule, operationalized with the indices that exist (the IMF's, the ILO's, the AIOE lineage — named, not "every index"), would on a per-person basis allocate more toward Boston than Jakarta. It fails on its own terms before any refinement.
- **Table 1 (centerpiece):** rows = US, Indonesia; columns = flagged exposure share → task filter (if the embed check keeps it) → adoption filter → realized-harm share → formal share of realized harm (composition) → cushion (UI coverage / safety-net access). Filters are scale factors on the flagged share under a stated uniformity assumption — the memo never calls them probabilities. Each cell sourced or flagged; denominators documented in the ledger.
- **US in two sentences:** large exposure, fast adoption, near-fully formal — large realized harm, met by the deepest cushions in the table: unemployment insurance, deep labor markets. The US is the reference pole, not the case.
- **Indonesia walk (the authority paragraph):** exposure figure is the emerging-market aggregate, said plainly; adoption slower; and the composition point — informality does not shield the country, it concentrates residual harm in formal urban clerical work, jobs that anchor middle-class incomes and carry no unemployment protection. *(Concentration sentence requires the sourced cross-tab per plan; fallback wording exists if it cannot be sourced — flag to author, do not silently cut.)*
- **The harm–need inversion, stated flatly (the section's product), in the variant the evidence supports — two locked wordings, chosen by whether the cross-tab was sourced, logged:** *Full variant (cross-tab found):* across the two rows, the ranking of realized harm tracks the ranking of cushions — a harm rule points toward the better-cushioned country — and within Indonesia the residual falls on the segment that is better-cushioned only relatively, still far below any US coverage. *Fallback variant (no cross-tab):* realized harm is largest where any cushion exists at all; within Indonesia, what cushion exists does not cover the concentrated residual. Never claim the Indonesian formal residual is "well-cushioned."
- **The liability rejoinder, one sentence, immediately after:** on a strict compensation view, harm is owed for regardless of the victim's insurance — but a dividend is not a court settlement; it is a fixed pool allocated once, and directing it toward those already insured against the harm leaves the uninsured with nothing. That answers the strongest philosophical objection without reopening the commons-vs-compensation debate the memo has scoped out.
- **Scenario sentence (the only sensitivity in the section):** under cautious, central, and fast assumptions the inversion holds; the appendix prints all three.

## §3 — Failure two: delivery caps arrival, and the mechanism that answers it (1¼ pages)

**Opening sentence:** "Even the population baseline meets its hardest problem here: what is allocated arrives only through identification and payment rails, and those are weakest where the largest per-capita claims sit."

- **Setup, two sentences:** collection at source; the chain runs per $100 allocated *to Indonesia* under the population rule. One sentence disowns the circular alternative: using §2's harm estimate as an allocation key would allocate by the very number §2 shows cannot rank countries. *(No appendix table for the variant — the sentence is the whole point.)*
- **The delivery chain, in words (one formula box, words not symbols):** arrival = share of people with foundational ID × share of those who can actually receive a payment × share of initiated payments that complete. Not targeting accuracy — a universal dividend has no targeting problem; the failure modes are identification, payment access, and payment completion. Components and sources per plan.
- **Counterpart scoping, one sentence:** the chain assumes a willing, competent recipient counterpart; where none exists the gap is larger and the instrument changes, not the principle.
- **The hard-test sentence** (contingent on verification — Indonesia must beat the LMIC median on both components): "Indonesia is chosen because its delivery system is good for its income level, so the gap measured here is a floor." Locked fallback if the medians say otherwise: "a typical-to-good LMIC system still loses a material share of a universal dividend — and the median system loses more."
- **The comparator, one sentence:** the median low-income and lower-middle-income country's figures on the same components (medians, not a picked case), one appendix row.
- **The legitimacy gap (the analysis's closing beat):** at current rails, the moral allocation and the effective allocation diverge — $100 owed is not $100 delivered, and the shortfall is largest for the populations with the strongest claim. That is the Trust's own problem: a dividend that visibly under-delivers to its intended beneficiaries undermines the case for the tax itself.
- **The mechanism (the memo's product, 5–6 sentences, flagged as a design sketch, not the design):** allocate by population; hold the shares of countries below a reachability threshold T in escrow — as a sketch rule, T = the lower-middle-income median reachability the memo already computes; escrowed principal is held by an existing multilateral trustee with public accounting, never by the payer, and is preserved: identification and payment rails are funded from the interest alone, through existing multilateral channels — not aid, but infrastructure the dividend's legitimacy requires under any allocation rule. Release turns on independently measured reachability (the same two observable components the memo measures), never on policy conditionality; and if a country has not cleared T within a fixed horizon, its principal releases anyway through the best available channel — the escrow secures the claim, it cannot extinguish it.

## §4 — What would change this (½ page)

**Opening sentence:** "The recommendation survives all three scenarios; what would change it is not a better estimate of how big the harm is, but a way to measure how it differs between countries."

- **Ledger pointer, two sentences (full ledger in appendix):** every number traces to a public ledger with value, source, denominator, and status; the flagged working figures are quarantined to the filters — exposure, formality, identification, and payment access are solidly sourced and visibly anchor the tables.
- **Two parameters, kept plainly apart:** (a) the *level* of harm — how much measured AI capability becomes real workplace impact. The memo's figures here are transparent second-best judgments in the absence of formal estimates; my Bayesian hierarchical meta-analysis (study-coding phase, ~21 studies, benchmark → lab → field transmission) is the instrument for the first-best number, and when it reports, that figure replaces the working value with no other change to the machinery. It sets the stakes and the urgency. (b) the *cross-country differentials* — the filters that decide who is hurt where. These are the open empirical frontier this memo names but does not close, and they, not the level, are what the allocation question turns on.
- **The trigger, three sentences (do now / watch / change):** do now — population allocation, escrow, rails. Watch — reachability trends, and the emergence of measurement for harm that runs through channels no exposure index can see: automated outsourcing, the erosion of the labor-arbitrage development path, demand and terms-of-trade effects on countries that never had the exposed jobs. Change — a harm tilt becomes defensible only if such non-exposure-channel measurement shows harm strong enough to reverse the inversion of §2 *and* reachability clears T broadly; better exposure-based measurement alone would deepen the inversion and confirm the recommendation, not overturn it.

## Global exposition rules (binding)

1. Plain declarative sentences; no consulting register; no formal notation in main text — filters are "scale factors," never probabilities.
2. Every section and paragraph opens with its conclusion.
3. All sensitivity is the three named scenarios (cautious / central / fast), defined once. No other ranges anywhere.
4. The chain appears once in words (§1); the delivery formula once in words (§3); numbers live in tables.
5. No ranking-flip claim; no point-estimate arrival number; no unsupported comparatives in the recommendation ("cheaper than", "highest-leverage" are banned — the claim is "needed under any allocation rule, and the one investment that buys legitimacy under all of them").
6. Weak numbers flagged in the sentence that uses them, never in an end-caveats section.
7. The memo never argues against the dividend; every such sentence is rewritten as "here is what makes it deliver."
8. The summary leads with the sequencing finding and concedes early, once, that the reader may already hold the allocation conclusion — the product is the reason and the sequence.
9. Escrow sentences are framed as securing the claim and building its delivery — never as withholding. The money is theirs; the mechanism makes it reachable. Any sentence readable as "held back until they qualify" is rewritten.
10. The §2 scenario sentence ("the inversion holds under cautious, central, and fast") may only be drafted after the scripts confirm it in all three scenario outputs; if it fails in any, the sentence reports which scenarios preserve it.
